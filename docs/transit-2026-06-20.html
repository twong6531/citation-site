West Hollywood Still Hasn't Filed Its SB 79 Housing Plan as the July 1 Deadline Closes In

June 15 – 17, 2026

SB 79 takes effect July 1, requiring denser housing near qualifying transit stops unless cities adopt their own Transit-Oriented Development Alternative Plan (TODAP) that matches the law's housing-capacity targets. Beverly Hills has already filed a TODAP; West Hollywood has filed nothing, and its mayor punted a vote to June. A new op-ed argues the city is running out of runway.

Key Facts

  • SB 79 takes effect July 1, 2026
  • A TODAP lets cities steer required density to commercial corridors instead of single-family blocks, as long as total capacity matches SB 79's mandate
  • Beverly Hills has filed a TODAP; West Hollywood has filed none
  • SCAG's draft SB 79 transit-stop map does not currently include West Hollywood's three planned K Line station areas — a possible, temporary reprieve

Why it matters: SB 79 is the most direct legal link between SoCal transit infrastructure and housing policy, and West Hollywood's inaction is becoming a test case for whether cities can quietly wait out the mandate via map technicalities rather than planning for it.

How LAX Built a $421M Concourse a Mile From the Terminal — Then Moved It

June 2026 (feature)

LAX engineers built the Midfield Satellite Concourse South components at a remote fabrication yard 1.5 to 1.75 miles from the active airfield, far enough to avoid disrupting runways and taxiways, then transported the finished structure into place — an approach now drawing attention as a model for airport expansion without operational shutdowns.

Key Facts

  • $421 million Midfield Satellite Concourse South, extending the West Gates of the Tom Bradley International Terminal
  • Added 8 narrowbody gates and 150,000 square feet of terminal space
  • First commercial flight: September 30, 2025; opened to the public October 21, 2025
  • Connected to the main terminal via an automated people-mover system

Why it matters: The remote-build technique offers a template for expanding LAX's gate capacity ahead of the 2028 Olympics without the runway and taxiway disruptions that typically come with terminal construction.
