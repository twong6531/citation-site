San Diego MTS Passes $482M Budget While Fare-Hike Fight Drags On

June 10 – 18, 2026

The San Diego Metropolitan Transit System board approved a $482.5 million FY2027 operating budget Thursday, a 2.4% increase that maintains current service levels but leans heavily on new revenue and cost-cutting to close a structural gap. The vote lands in the middle of a separate, contentious push to raise rider fares as much as 40% by fall — a plan the board is simultaneously trying to soften for low-income riders.

Key Facts

  • $482.5 million FY2027 MTS operating budget, up 2.4% year over year
  • MTS faces a $94 million deficit next fiscal year, growing to over $122 million by 2030
  • Fares could rise as much as 40% by October 1, 2026 if the Regional Fare Ordinance changes are adopted
  • Board separately advanced a motion to freeze fares for riders enrolled in CalFresh, CalWorks, or Medi-Cal

Why it matters: San Diego is staring down the same structural funding cliff hitting Metrolink and LA Metro — rising costs and flat farebox revenue colliding with a board reluctant to cut service. How MTS balances the budget against a 40% fare hike will be a test case for transit agencies statewide.

D Line Ridership Jumps 62% as World Cup Supercharges the Rail Network

June 16 – 17, 2026

Metro says systemwide rail ridership is up 9.5% year over year — the strongest stretch in more than six years — driven by a 62% jump on the D Line since three new Wilshire-corridor stations opened in early May, plus a fresh boost from World Cup match traffic. Weekend rail ridership rose 18% as fans and tourists used trains to reach venues, dining, and entertainment districts.

Key Facts

  • Systemwide rail ridership up 9.5% year over year
  • D Line ridership up 62% since May station openings
  • K Line up 48%, with weekend gains over 60%; A Line up 11.3%; B Line up 7%
  • Weekend rail ridership overall up 18%
  • Metro CEO Stephanie Wiggins: D Line "cuts commute times in half"

Why it matters: This is Metro's strongest ridership data point in years and the clearest evidence yet that World Cup-driven service investments are paying off — a case study Metro will lean on heading into the 2028 Olympics.

California High-Speed Rail Awards $3.5B Track and Systems Contract

June 1 – 15, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority board approved a $3.5 billion contract with a Kiewit-led consortium (Kiewit, Stacey & Witbeck, Herzog) to install track, electrification, and train control systems on the 190-km Central Valley starter segment — a milestone the Authority is calling the launch of the nation's first true high-speed rail construction. Tracklaying is expected to begin before year's end, even as the project's overall price tag continues to draw scrutiny.

Key Facts

  • $3.5 billion contract awarded to a Kiewit-led consortium
  • Covers 190 km (118 miles) of Central Valley alignment
  • Includes track, overhead contact lines, train control and communications systems
  • Trains designed to reach 355 km/h (220 mph)
  • Tracklaying anticipated to begin before the end of 2026

Why it matters: This is the most concrete construction milestone yet for the project, arriving as critics continue to hammer the program's now-$231 billion total cost estimate and uncertain federal funding.

June 10 – 12, 2026

Metrolink launched a six-month contactless fare payment pilot on the San Bernardino Line and Arrow service, letting riders tap a credit card, debit card, mobile wallet, or wearable instead of using ticket machines or the mobile app. The pilot, backed by SCAG and Caltrans, caps daily fares at the cost of a SoCal Day Pass and is timed to ease travel for World Cup visitors connecting to Metro's SoFi Stadium bus service.

Key Facts

  • Connects riders to Metro's special World Cup bus service to SoFi Stadium

Why it matters: It's Metrolink's first real step toward modern fare payment and a direct response to the same World Cup demand spike fueling Metro's ridership numbers.
