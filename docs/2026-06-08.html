Subject Research

Teenage Engineering's APC-2 is a precision vinyl record cutting lathe — an electromechanical device that engraves audio waveforms as modulated spiral grooves into soft lacquer blanks. The diamond-tipped cutting stylus is mounted on a precision linear rail that advances exactly 0.3mm per platter revolution, carving groove walls only 25 micrometers wide. The turntable platter spins at 33 or 45 RPM; the stylus vibrates laterally and vertically at audio frequencies simultaneously (the 45° stereo groove standard), creating the groove geometry that a playback stylus will later trace to reproduce sound. Previously, cutting lathes required six-figure studio investment — Neumann and Scully machines ran $50,000–$250,000. The APC-2 brings this process to independent artists and audiophile engineers at a fraction of that cost.
