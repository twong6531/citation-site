HIGH LA Metro

New D Line Stations Help Drive Strongest Rail Ridership in Six-Plus Years

May 2026 ridership; published June 16, 2026

Metro's own recap of its record May ridership month adds new World Cup detail to numbers first reported earlier this week: nearly 45,000 rides were taken on dedicated World Cup service to and from the first two matches at SoFi Stadium, run across 15 locations in LA and Orange counties in partnership with 11 regional transit agencies. Metro is calling it an unprecedented mobilization for a single sporting event.

Key Facts

  • Rail ridership hit its highest level in more than six years in May 2026, with 6.47M+ rail trips, up 9.5% year-over-year
  • D Line ridership up 62% YoY (weekends up nearly 80%) following the May 8 opening of three new stations
  • ~45,000 rides taken on special World Cup transit service to the first two SoFi Stadium matches
  • Service spanned 15 locations across LA and Orange counties with 11 regional transit partners
  • Metro describes the coordination as an unprecedented number of service locations mobilized for a single sporting event

Why it matters: Metro is explicitly fusing its strongest ridership data in years with World Cup operations to build the case that infrastructure investment plus mega-event demand can permanently shift travel behavior toward rail.

MEDIUM LA Metro

How Metro Transformed Nearly 200 Buses Into Rolling World Cup Celebrations

Published June 22, 2026

As the official Public Transit Provider of LA's World Cup 2026 host committee, Metro wrapped 196 buses in custom World Cup designs, distributing them across all 10 bus divisions so riders systemwide — not just those near stadium venues — encounter the tournament branding. Each 40-foot bus required just over 1,000 square feet of vinyl to wrap both sides and the rear.

Key Facts

  • 196 buses wrapped in World Cup branding, spanning all 10 of Metro's bus divisions
  • Each 40-foot bus needed just over 1,000 sq. ft. of vinyl wrap material
  • Wrapped buses circulate on regular routes systemwide, not confined to stadium-adjacent corridors
  • Part of Metro's role as official Public Transit Provider for the Los Angeles World Cup 2026 host committee

Why it matters: A low-cost, high-visibility branding push that turns Metro's everyday fleet into ambient World Cup marketing — and a dry run for similarly themed fleet branding Metro will likely repeat for the 2028 Olympics.

HIGH LA Metro

LA Metro Board Approves Nearly $10 Billion FY27 Budget

Approved May 28, 2026; takes effect July 1, 2026

Metro's Board approved a $9.7 billion budget for fiscal year 2027, a 2.4% increase over the prior year, funding both day-to-day operations and a wave of rail expansion including the East San Fernando Valley light rail line and the NoHo-Pasadena BRT, which broke ground the same week. The budget also trims Metro's subsidy to Metrolink amid that agency's deepening funding crisis.

Key Facts

  • Total FY27 budget: $9.7 billion, up 2.4% year-over-year, effective July 1, 2026
  • ~$3 billion for operations and maintenance; ~$2 billion for expansion projects; $680M+ for D Line extension construction
  • East San Fernando Valley light rail: $3.999 billion life-of-project budget approved — 6.5 miles, 11 stations along Van Nuys Blvd. connecting to the G Line
  • NoHo-Pasadena BRT broke ground: 19 miles, 22 stations across Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and LA
  • Metro's subsidy to Metrolink cut by roughly $6 million (3%) amid Metrolink's own ~$30M shortfall
  • Mayor Karen Bass set to chair the Metro Board starting July 2026

Why it matters: This budget is Metro's clearest statement yet that it's doubling down on rail expansion even as it tightens support for Metrolink — setting up a structural tension between LA's two largest rail operators heading into the Olympics buildout.

MEDIUM LAX/LAWA

LAX's Long-Awaited SkyLink Train Nears the Finish Line as Testing Continues

Testing began April 22, 2026; ongoing as of late June 2026

SkyLink, LAX's automated people mover, has been running a full passenger-free test schedule since April 22 along its 2.25-mile, six-station elevated guideway, with trains departing every two minutes. The system needs 30 consecutive issue-free days before any volunteer or public rides begin — a milestone years behind the original 2023 target.

Key Facts

  • Testing phase began April 22, 2026, expected to run roughly 60 days on a full operating schedule
  • Route: 2.25-mile elevated guideway with six stations, trains every 2 minutes, ≤10-minute ride to Tom Bradley International Terminal
  • Requires 30 consecutive days without major issues before any passenger rides begin
  • Projected to carry 30 million-plus riders annually once open; part of a $5.5 billion LAX modernization program
  • Construction began in 2019; original 2023 opening missed due to contractor disputes with LAX Integrated Express Solutions

Why it matters: SkyLink is the missing link connecting LAX directly to Metro rail — its opening, whenever it finally comes, will reshape airport access for both the remaining World Cup matches and the 2028 Olympics.

HIGH CA High-Speed Rail

California High-Speed Rail Costs Soar to $231 Billion

Reported April 30, 2026; ongoing debate through June 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority's latest cost estimate has ballooned to $231 billion, roughly 700% above the original $33-45 billion 2008 voter-approved projection. The Trump administration has pulled $4 billion in federal funding, and state lawmakers are increasingly questioning whether the project will ever be completed.

Key Facts

  • Current cost estimate: $231 billion, up from an original $33-45 billion (2008) — roughly a 700% overrun
  • Trump administration withdrew $4 billion in federal funding; the state subsequently dropped its recovery lawsuit
  • Phase 1 (Central Valley, Merced-Bakersfield, 171 miles) now not expected complete until 2032, 12 years past the original 2020 target
  • State Sen. Tony Strickland called it "the most wasteful government project in probably world history," predicting it "will never be built"

Why it matters: The cost spiral directly threatens the LA-Anaheim extension now under environmental review, and gives critics fresh ammunition just as the Authority tries to advance the Southern California segment of the statewide system.

MEDIUM CA High-Speed Rail

High-Speed Rail Authority Advances Draft Environmental Document for LA-Anaheim Section

Released December 5, 2025; comment period closed February 3, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority's Draft EIR/EIS for the 30-mile Los Angeles Union Station-to-Anaheim corridor remains the key planning document for the Southern California segment, even as statewide cost overruns dominate headlines. The preferred alternative shares track with existing passenger rail through 14 cities and could add an intermediate station near Norwalk or Fullerton.

Key Facts

  • Corridor: 30 miles from LA Union Station to Anaheim, passing through 14 cities
  • Preferred alternative: "Shared Passenger Track Alternative A," with a Light Maintenance Facility planned at 26th Street
  • Possible intermediate station at Norwalk/Santa Fe Springs or the Fullerton Metrolink/Amtrak station
  • Statewide system: 494 miles planned San Francisco-to-Anaheim; 463 miles environmentally cleared, 171 miles in design or construction
  • ~16,400 jobs created to date, with up to 1,700 workers on site daily

Why it matters: This is the document that determines whether and how high-speed rail ever reaches Los Angeles and Orange counties — its fate is now entangled with the statewide cost crisis and shrinking federal support.

LOW-MEDIUM Amtrak Pacific Surfliner

Pacific Surfliner's 13th Daily Roundtrip Keeps Paying Off Through World Cup Season

Added January 26, 2026; in effect through World Cup season

Amtrak and the LOSSAN Rail Corridor Agency's addition of a 13th daily roundtrip between Los Angeles and San Diego, funded by a federal Restoration & Enhancement grant, restored pre-pandemic frequency on the corridor just in time to absorb World Cup-driven travel demand between the two metro areas this month.

Key Facts

  • 13th daily roundtrip began January 26, 2026 on the 351-mile, 29-station LA-San Diego corridor
  • Funded by a $27.1 million FRA Restoration & Enhancement grant
  • Total service now: 13 LA-San Diego roundtrips, 5 San Diego-Goleta, 2 full-corridor San Diego-San Luis Obispo daily roundtrips
  • LOSSAN officials called the expansion a "milestone" in restoring pre-pandemic service levels

Why it matters: The Surfliner's restored frequency is one of the few SoCal rail success stories untouched by funding crisis — and gives World Cup travelers a viable alternative to I-5 between the two host markets.

HIGH Metrolink

Metrolink's "Temporary" Service Cuts Show No Sign of Ending

Cuts began March 23, 2026; reported continuing as of May 7, 2026

Metrolink's roughly 20% service reduction across six of its seven lines, originally framed as temporary when imposed in March, will continue indefinitely as the agency grapples with diesel-locomotive parts shortages and a structural funding shortfall. Member agencies including LA Metro and OCTA are now trimming their own subsidies, deepening the squeeze just as Metrolink tries to serve World Cup-related travel.

Key Facts

  • Service cuts of roughly 20% across 6 of 7 Metrolink lines took effect March 23, 2026, and remain in place with no end date
  • Annual funding shortfall estimated at $15 million, on top of a roughly $30 million total deficit
  • Ridership remains at roughly 58% of pre-pandemic levels as of early 2026
  • Member agencies could cut Metrolink funding by another $10 million; LA Metro's FY27 budget already trims its subsidy ~3%
  • A fare increase is under consideration as early as October 2026; further service cuts of up to 40% have been floated

Why it matters: Metrolink's slow-motion crisis is the clearest counterpoint to Metro's World Cup ridership boom — proof that mega-event enthusiasm hasn't solved the region's deeper commuter-rail funding problems.

MEDIUM SCAG

SCAG Unveils Draft Map for SB 79 Transit-Housing Law as LA Seeks a Delay

SCAG draft released June 1, 2026; SB 79 takes effect July 1, 2026

The Southern California Association of Governments released its draft implementation map for SB 79, the state law boosting housing density near transit stops, setting up height allowances of up to nine stories near heavy-rail stations. Los Angeles is simultaneously moving to delay the law's reach in certain neighborhoods, highlighting friction between regional housing mandates and local control just before the law takes effect.

Key Facts

  • SCAG's draft map: Tier 1 (heavy rail) allows up to 9 stories within 200 feet of a station; Tier 2 (light rail/BRT) allows 8/6/5 stories at 200 feet, a quarter-mile, and a half-mile
  • Public comment on the draft closed June 12, 2026; SCAG's Regional Council vote is set for July 2, 2026
  • SB 79 itself takes effect July 1, 2026
  • LA City Council has moved to delay implementation for roughly 55 transit stops, capping density at 4 stories in those zones

Why it matters: How SCAG and individual cities resolve this tension will determine whether SB 79 meaningfully reshapes density around the rail network that Metro is simultaneously expanding and marketing during the World Cup.

Why it matters: OCTA's fiscal discipline contrasts with Metrolink's deepening crisis, even though OCTA is itself a Metrolink funding partner — underscoring how unevenly the region's transit agencies are weathering the post-pandemic ridership recovery.

Emerging Themes

Metro's World Cup operation is now a full-spectrum marketing and capacity campaign

Today's Metro stories — record D Line ridership, 196 wrapped buses, and the Union Station Fan Zone — show an agency treating the World Cup as both a stress test and a branding opportunity. The throughline from infrastructure (new D Line stations) to fleet branding to event programming suggests a deliberate, coordinated push that will template Metro's 2028 Olympics strategy.

A widening gap between Metro's gains and Metrolink's crisis

Metro posted its strongest ridership month in six years and a $9.7 billion budget with expansion funding intact, while Metrolink's "temporary" 20% service cuts show no end date and member agencies — including Metro itself — are trimming Metrolink's subsidies further. The region's two largest rail systems are now moving in opposite directions even as both serve World Cup travelers.

High-speed rail's cost crisis threatens to swallow the Southern California extension

The $231 billion statewide cost estimate and withdrawn federal funding cast a shadow over the LA-Anaheim Draft Environmental Document, which remains the key planning step for ever bringing high-speed rail into Los Angeles and Orange counties. Without resolving the funding picture, the environmental review itself may become moot.

SB 79's rollout exposes housing-versus-local-control tension right as it takes effect

SCAG's draft density map allowing up to nine stories near heavy-rail stations lands the same month LA moves to delay the law in dozens of station areas — a preview of the friction likely to recur across SoCal as cities balance state housing mandates against local opposition near the same rail lines Metro is expanding.

Mid-sized agencies are managing fiscal discipline differently

OCTA's balanced FY27 budget and the Surfliner's restored 13th daily roundtrip show that not every SoCal operator is in crisis — but both budgets explicitly cite Metrolink's funding troubles as an ongoing risk they must help absorb, tying every agency's fortunes back to the region's shared commuter-rail backbone.
