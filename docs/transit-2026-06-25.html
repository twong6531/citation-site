HIGH LAX/LAWA

As World Cup Fans Pour Into LAX, SkyLink Still Isn't Carrying Passengers

Reported June 22 – 25, 2026

Three years past its original 2023 opening date, LAX's automated people mover remains stuck in passenger-free testing even as World Cup travelers flood the airport. The train must run 30 consecutive days without a problem before it can open to riders — a bar it has not yet cleared — leaving fans to rely on aging shuttle buses and ride-share lines instead.

Key Facts

  • SkyLink must complete 30 consecutive issue-free days of testing before accepting any passengers — not yet achieved
  • System contractor LINXS now projects passenger service won't begin until at least early October 2026
  • A $36 million dispute between LAWA and LINXS over repair-work liability is a central factor in the delay
  • Once open: 2.25-mile, six-station guideway with trains every 2 minutes at peak, ≤10-minute ride to Tom Bradley International Terminal
  • World Cup visitors are left depending on shuttle buses and ride-share for airport access during the tournament's busiest weeks

Why it matters: SkyLink's continued absence during the actual World Cup — not just leading up to it — is the clearest sign yet that LAX will miss its highest-profile deadline, raising the stakes for whether it's ready before the 2028 Olympics.

HIGH CA High-Speed Rail

California High-Speed Rail Taps Global Private Investment Consortium

Announced June 24, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority signed a Co-Development Agreement with Momentum Alliance Partners — a consortium including Plenary Americas, CDPQ Infra, Keolis, SNCF Voyageurs, Jacobs, Arup and others — to evaluate financing and delivery strategies for expanding the system beyond the Central Valley segment now under construction. It marks the Authority's most concrete step yet toward bringing private capital into the project.

Key Facts

  • Co-Development Agreement signed June 24, 2026 with Momentum Alliance Partners
  • Consortium includes Plenary Americas, CDPQ Infra, Keolis, SNCF Voyageurs, Jacobs, Sener, SYSTRA, Setec, Arup, and Steer
  • Initial six-month predevelopment and evaluation phase covering financing, delivery models, and operations beyond Merced-Bakersfield
  • Consortium will also assess commercialization opportunities: broadband/fiber, data infrastructure, and station-area development
  • Follows a December 2025 process the Authority launched specifically to draw in private investors

Why it matters: With statewide costs estimated as high as $231 billion and federal funding pulled, private capital is now central to whether high-speed rail ever extends from the Central Valley into Los Angeles and Orange counties.

HIGH SCAG

Housing Advocates Say SCAG's SB 79 Methodology Undermines the Law's Mandate

June 2026 (public comment closed June 12; Regional Council vote July 2)

A coalition of housing advocates is pushing back on SCAG's draft SB 79 transit-density map, arguing its methodology for calculating allowable building heights near rail and bus-rapid-transit stops waters down the law's intent to spur transit-oriented housing. The dispute comes as SCAG prepares to finalize its approach ahead of a July 2 Regional Council vote, just before SB 79 takes effect statewide on July 1.

Key Facts

  • SCAG's draft SB 79 map currently covers LA County only, the region's sole "urban transit county" until the OC Streetcar opens
  • Public comment period on the draft map closed June 12, 2026
  • SCAG's Regional Council is set to act on the final methodology July 2, 2026, one day after SB 79 takes effect statewide
  • Housing advocates argue SCAG's calculation method shrinks the area and density actually unlocked near stations compared to the law's intent
  • SCAG must address mapping corrections by the statutory deadline of July 1, 2026

Why it matters: The fight over SCAG's methodology will determine how much new housing SB 79 actually unlocks near the rail and BRT lines Metro is simultaneously expanding — a fight playing out in the law's final weeks before taking effect.

HIGH Metrolink

LA Metro and OCTA Propose Deeper Metrolink Subsidy Cuts Through 2030

Ongoing through 2026; cuts proposed through 2030

Member agencies LA Metro and OCTA have proposed reducing their combined operations support for Metrolink by $9.5 million annually, on an ongoing basis through 2030 — a structural cut layered on top of Metrolink's existing post-pandemic funding shortfall. Advocates warn the planned 2028 expansion of "Metrolink Reimagined" weekend service may now never happen without new revenue.

Key Facts

  • LA Metro and OCTA propose cutting combined operations support by $9.5 million annually, ongoing through 2030
  • Metrolink saw the second-highest ridership growth rate of any U.S. regional rail agency in 2024-2025, behind only Caltrain
  • Metrolink broke its all-time weekend ridership record in March 2025, despite the funding pressure
  • Planned 2028 weekend expansion of "Metrolink Reimagined" service is now at risk without additional revenue
  • Without member-agency support, lines could revert to peak-only schedules

Why it matters: Metrolink's strongest ridership growth in years is colliding directly with its largest funders pulling back support — a structural governance problem advocates say threatens to undo recent gains just as regional rail demand is rising.

MEDIUM LA Metro

'Beyond Our Expectations': Metro's New Transit Police Force Tops 2,400 Applicants

Recruitment launched May 28; applicant count as of June 11, 2026

Metro's Department of Public Safety recruitment drive, led by former San Francisco police chief William Scott, has drawn 2,468 applicants in just over two weeks — nearly four times the 630 sworn officers the agency plans to hire. Competitive pay, with entry-level officers earning up to $132,499 a year, is helping fuel interest in the agency's first in-house police force.

Key Facts

  • 2,468 applicants as of June 11, up from 950 in the recruitment's first 24 hours on May 28
  • Metro plans to hire a maximum of 630 sworn officers — roughly four applicants per opening
  • Entry-level officer pay up to $132,499/year; lateral officers with experience up to $142,521/year
  • Recruitment led by William Scott, former San Francisco police chief, hired a year ago to head Metro's Department of Public Safety
  • Full deployment of Metro's own sworn force targeted by the end of 2029

Why it matters: Overwhelming applicant interest gives Metro confidence to move fast on building an in-house police force — a major bet on improving rider safety perceptions ahead of the 2028 Olympics.

MEDIUM LA Metro

Free GoPass Transit Tops 500,000 LA County Students, Nearly 60 Million Boardings

GoPass Year 6 runs July 1, 2026 – September 30, 2027

More than 500,000 K-12, adult-school, and community college students across LA County now ride Metro buses and trains for free through the GoPass program, which has logged nearly 60 million student boardings since launching in 2021. The program's sixth year kicks off July 1, extending fareless transit access just as Metro pushes ridership growth across the system.

Key Facts

  • More than 500,000 students participate in GoPass across LA County K-12, adult schools, and community colleges
  • Nearly 60 million student boardings recorded since the program launched in 2021
  • GoPass Year 6 is valid July 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027
  • Students with a valid school ID also get free rides on Metrolink and transfers to OCTA under a complementary regional pass program

Why it matters: Five years of fareless access has built a generation of habitual transit riders in LA County — a long-term ridership base Metro is counting on well beyond the World Cup bump.

Pacific Surfliner Requires Advance Reservations Through World Cup Travel Surge

Reservation requirement in effect June 11 – July 19, 2026

With FIFA World Cup matches drawing heavy travel demand along the LA-San Diego corridor, Amtrak is requiring date- and time-specific advance reservations on Pacific Surfliner trains from June 11 through July 19 — a departure from the route's normal walk-up flexibility. The restriction comes even as the line runs a record 13 daily roundtrips between the two cities.

Key Facts

  • Advance reservations required for travel June 11 – July 19, 2026 on the LA-San Diego segment
  • Surfliner now runs 13 daily roundtrips between LA and San Diego, restoring pre-pandemic frequency
  • Full route spans 351 miles through San Diego, Orange, LA, Ventura, Santa Barbara, and San Luis Obispo counties
  • Trains offer reclining seats, power outlets, Wi-Fi, and a Market Café with local wine and craft beer

Why it matters: Forcing reservations on a normally flexible commuter-style service shows just how much World Cup demand is straining Southern California's rail capacity — a preview of the load the system will face again for the 2028 Olympics.

LOW-MEDIUM OCTA

OCTA Board Formally Adopts $2 Billion Budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27

Adopted June 2026; takes effect July 1, 2026

The OCTA Board of Directors has formally approved a balanced roughly $2 billion budget for FY 2026-27, finalizing the proposal advanced earlier this spring. Transit investments account for about 52% of spending, split across OC Bus operations, regional rail, local rail, and Measure M-funded transit programs, with no draws required from unplanned reserves.

Key Facts

  • Adopted budget totals approximately $2 billion, effective July 1, 2026
  • 52% of the budget is dedicated to transit: 34% bus operations, 13% regional rail, 2% local rail, 2% Measure M transit programs
  • No unplanned reserve draws needed to balance the budget
  • Budget supports OCTA's express lane network and continued Measure M voter commitments

Why it matters: OCTA's formal adoption locks in transit funding levels for the year just as its Metrolink subsidy decisions ripple through the regional rail system's deepening funding fight.

Emerging Themes

The World Cup is exposing the gap between SoCal transit's marketing and its capacity

LAX's SkyLink still can't carry a single passenger, Pacific Surfliner now requires reservations, and Metrolink is running special late-night service on a shoestring funding base — all while Metro keeps directing fans toward rail. The tournament is stress-testing infrastructure that, in several cases, simply isn't ready.

Metrolink's funding fight is intensifying, not stabilizing

Member agencies LA Metro and OCTA are now proposing to cut Metrolink support by $9.5 million a year through 2030 — even as the agency posts some of the strongest ridership growth of any regional rail system nationally and runs expanded World Cup service. The mismatch between ridership momentum and funding support is becoming structural.

Big infrastructure bets are turning toward private capital and private advocacy fights

California High-Speed Rail's new private investment consortium and the housing-advocate fight over SCAG's SB 79 methodology both show major regional transit and housing policy increasingly being shaped by outside capital and outside pressure groups, not just agency boards.

Metro's safety and ridership investments are compounding

A 2,400-plus applicant pool for Metro's new transit police force and 500,000-plus students riding free under GoPass both reflect an agency betting heavily on long-term rider trust and habit-formation — investments that extend well past the World Cup's short-term ridership spike.
