HIGH LA Metro

D Line Stations Push Metro Rail Ridership to Six-Year High as World Cup Service Rolls On

May 2026 – June 16, 2026

Metro Rail ridership hit its highest level since before the COVID-19 pandemic in May, a surge the agency credits to the opening of three new D Line stations serving Miracle Mile, the Fairfax District and Beverly Hills-adjacent neighborhoods. Metro says the momentum has carried into June, with World Cup-dedicated bus and rail service to SoFi Stadium starting strong in its first week of matches.

Key Facts

  • Metro Rail ridership reached its highest level in more than six years in May 2026
  • Three new D Line stations opened roughly a month earlier, serving Miracle Mile, Fairfax and Beverly Hills-adjacent areas
  • World Cup transit service includes 15 dedicated bus/rail locations feeding SoFi Stadium
  • Metro and partner agencies are running direct bus service from Anaheim, Newport Beach and other regional hubs

Why it matters: The D Line opening is the clearest evidence yet that new rail infrastructure converts directly into ridership — a data point Metro will lean on heavily as it lobbies for 2028 Olympics transit funding.

Connects to: $1.75 World Cup shuttle plan; East LA E Line extension debate.

HIGH LA Metro

East LA Residents Weigh a $7.9 Billion E Line Extension to Montebello

June 13, 2026

Metro is advancing a 4.7-mile E Line extension that would connect East Los Angeles to Montebello with four new stations, relocating the existing Atlantic and Pomona stations underground. Residents are split — some want a longer public comment window and more transparency on underground infrastructure after a pipeline was punctured during construction in late May, while others say the line will be transformative for car-free riders.

Key Facts

  • Extension cost estimated at $7.9 billion for 4.7 miles and four new stations
  • Construction would begin in 2029 and run 8–10 years, pending full funding
  • Project is the first phase of a longer-term plan to extend the E Line to Whittier
  • Residents are requesting a 90-day public comment window following a May pipeline strike during nearby construction

Why it matters: The project is a test case for how Metro balances aggressive rail expansion with neighborhood trust in a corridor still recovering from construction mishaps.

Connects to: D Line ridership record; Metro's broader rail expansion push ahead of 2028.

MEDIUM LA Metro Dept. of Public Safety

Metro's New In-House Police Force Tops 2,400 Applicants in Weeks

June 9 – 15, 2026

Metro's recruitment drive for its own Department of Public Safety has drawn 2,400 applications in the weeks since launch — far beyond initial projections — as the agency builds out a roughly 400-officer force to replace contracted LAPD and Sheriff's Department patrols. Chief Bill Scott, the former San Francisco police chief, says many applicants are coming from other departments, plus 500 current Metro employees.

Key Facts

  • 2,400 applicants in the weeks since recruitment launched, after 950 in the first 24 hours
  • New force targets roughly 400 sworn officers plus ambassadors and behavioral health specialists
  • Chief Bill Scott, former SFPD chief, hired in May 2025 to lead the department
  • 500 applications came from current Metro employees

Why it matters: Strong recruitment numbers give Metro cover to argue its in-house policing model — born from frustration with contracted LAPD/Sheriff patrols — can actually staff up on schedule.

Connects to: Accidental shooting on Metro bus in Ladera Heights; ongoing rider-safety concerns.

HIGH San Diego MTS

San Diego MTS Passes $482M Budget, Freezes Fares for Low-Income Riders

June 12 – 18, 2026

The San Diego Metropolitan Transit System board approved a $482.5 million FY2027 budget — a 2.4% increase that maintains current service levels — while separately advancing a plan to freeze fares for low-income riders ahead of an expected system-wide fare increase. The budget leans on advertising, energy credits and joint-development revenue to offset a relatively flat fare-revenue stream against rising costs.

Key Facts

  • Budget set at $482.5 million, up 2.4% year-over-year
  • MTS provides more than 250,000 weekday passenger trips
  • New revenue sources include advertising, energy credits and joint-development deals
  • Board separately advanced a fare freeze for low-income riders ahead of a system-wide fare hike

Why it matters: MTS is threading the same fiscal-cliff needle as transit agencies nationwide — raising fares to close budget gaps while trying not to price out the riders most dependent on service.

Connects to: MTS Clairemont parking expansion; statewide transit fiscal-cliff pressure.

HIGH CA High-Speed Rail

California High-Speed Rail Awards $3.5 Billion Track and Systems Contract

June 1 – 15, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority approved a $3.5 billion contract with a Kiewit-led consortium — including Stacy and Witbeck and Herzog — to install track, overhead electrification and train control systems on the 118-mile Central Valley starter segment. It marks the project's shift from civil construction to systems installation, with tracklaying expected to begin before year's end.

Key Facts

  • Contract value: $3.5 billion, awarded to a Kiewit/Stacy and Witbeck/Herzog consortium
  • Covers track, overhead contact lines, and train control/communications on the 118-mile Central Valley segment
  • System will be built for speeds up to 220 mph (355 km/h)
  • More than 80 miles of guideway and 60 major structures already complete as of early June

Why it matters: This is the first true track-and-systems contract in U.S. high-speed rail history — a concrete construction milestone that stands in contrast to the project's well-documented cost and funding turmoil.

Connects to: CA HSR's Central Valley data-center monetization plan and ongoing federal funding fight (covered in prior briefings).

MEDIUM Metrolink

Metrolink Debuts Contactless Payment Pilot Ahead of World Cup Crowds

June 11 – 19, 2026

Metrolink launched a six-month contactless payment pilot on the San Bernardino Line and Arrow service, letting riders between Redlands and Los Angeles tap a credit card, debit card or mobile wallet at station validators instead of using a ticket machine or app. The pilot, running through July 19, is aimed squarely at occasional and visiting riders during the World Cup.

Key Facts

  • Pilot covers the San Bernardino Line and Arrow service between Redlands and LA
  • Runs as a six-month trial through July 19, 2026
  • Accepts Visa, Mastercard, Discover, Amex, Apple Pay and Google Pay
  • Daily fare caps: $15 weekdays, $10 weekends — never more than a SoCal Day Pass

Why it matters: Contactless fare payment removes a major barrier for first-time and tourist riders — exactly the audience Metrolink needs to convert during a once-in-a-generation World Cup tourism spike.

Connects to: $1.75 Metro World Cup shuttle plan; statewide contactless-fare rollout.

MEDIUM LA Metro

$1.75 Metro Rides Undercut $200 SoFi Parking for World Cup Fans

June 12, 2026

Metro is running discounted $1.75 bus and rail rides to SoFi Stadium for World Cup matches, with service starting four hours before kickoff and continuing two hours after the final whistle. Fifteen shuttle stops feed the stadium, and thousands of Metro Ambassadors are stationed system-wide to help international visitors navigate fares and routes.

Key Facts

  • Fare: $1.75 each way on Metro Rail or Bus to SoFi Stadium
  • 15 shuttle stops mapped across the region
  • Service runs 4 hours before kickoff to 2 hours after the match ends
  • Transfers valid for 2 hours across bus-to-rail or rail-to-bus connections

Why it matters: Pricing transit at a fraction of stadium parking costs is Metro's clearest pitch yet to convert World Cup tourists into habitual riders — with the same playbook likely reused for the 2028 Olympics.

Connects to: D Line ridership record; Metrolink contactless payment pilot.

Emerging Themes

World Cup as a live transit stress test

Across Metro, Metrolink and LAX, the FIFA World Cup is functioning as an unplanned dry run for the 2028 Olympics — discounted fares, contactless payment pilots, dedicated shuttle networks and record ridership are all being deployed simultaneously, with agencies clearly using the tournament to build habits and infrastructure they'll lean on again in two years.

New rail infrastructure is visibly moving the ridership needle

Metro's D Line station openings translating directly into the highest rail ridership in six years gives transit advocates concrete evidence that capital investment pays off in usage — a data point likely to be cited heavily in the East LA E Line extension debate and future funding fights.

Transit agency budgets are squeezed but holding

San Diego MTS's 2.4% budget increase and fare-freeze maneuver for low-income riders is a small-scale preview of the "fiscal cliff" dynamic playing out at transit agencies statewide — rising costs, flat fare revenue, and a search for non-fare income like advertising and joint development.

CA High-Speed Rail's narrative is bifurcating

The $3.5 billion track-and-systems contract is the project's most concrete construction milestone to date, even as cost overruns and federal funding fights continue to dominate the project's public narrative — two very different stories running on parallel tracks, literally and figuratively.

Transit safety investment is becoming visible on the ground

Metro's transit police recruitment surge (2,400 applicants) is unfolding against a backdrop of real incidents — like the accidental shooting on a Ladera Heights bus — that the new Department of Public Safety will soon be tasked with responding to directly.
