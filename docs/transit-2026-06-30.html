HIGH CA High-Speed Rail

High-speed rail issues $2.4B Merced-Madera RFQ, lines up Plenary-led team to evaluate private financing

June 25 – June 29, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority board approved a Request for Qualifications for a roughly $2.4 billion design-build contract covering the 34-mile Merced-to-Madera civil works segment. Separately, the Authority finalized a Co-Development Agreement with Momentum Alliance Partners — a consortium led by Plenary Americas with CDPQ Infra, Keolis, SNCF Voyageurs, and Jacobs — to spend six months evaluating public-private partnership models for funding and delivering future expansion.

Key Facts

  • Merced-to-Madera civil works contract valued at approximately $2.4 billion, covering about 34 miles
  • Two qualified design-build teams to be shortlisted by end of 2026; major construction targeted for late 2027 through 2030
  • Momentum Alliance Partners, led by Plenary Americas, begins a six-month P3 financing evaluation
  • Future P3 financing could draw on up to roughly $1 billion through 2045 in securitized cap-and-invest Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund revenue
  • Active Central Valley construction spans 171 miles between Merced and Bakersfield, with over 80 miles of guideway and 61 major structures completed

Why it matters: Pairing a major new construction procurement with a private-financing evaluation signals the Authority is trying to both keep building and diversify away from pure state/federal funding — a dual-track strategy it will need as it simultaneously trims costs elsewhere on the line.

HIGH CA High-Speed Rail

High-speed rail drops downtown Bakersfield station, shifts north to save $1.7B

June 24 – June 25, 2026

The California High-Speed Rail Authority is abandoning its long-planned downtown Bakersfield station in favor of an "interim" site north of 7th Standard Road, about five miles from the original location near F Street and Golden State Avenue. The Authority says the new site offers strategic access to Meadows Field Airport while preserving a future track alignment toward downtown Bakersfield and Palmdale, but critics argue the original downtown plan was always going to be too costly to build.

Key Facts

  • New interim station site sits about five miles from the original downtown F Street/Golden State Avenue location
  • The shift is projected to save the Authority an estimated $1.7 billion
  • Media reports indicate the 7th Standard Road station may not open until 2033
  • Authority CEO Ian Choudri: "It's never about changing anything just because we want to," citing funding-driven design changes
  • Kern County Taxpayers Association chairman Michael Turnipseed called the original downtown plan "probably the worst public works project in the history of the United States"

Why it matters: Swapping a costly downtown alignment for a cheaper interim site north of the city is the clearest sign yet that the Authority is willing to scale back original promises to keep the broader system moving — a trade-off Bakersfield-area riders and officials will be litigating for years.

HIGH LA Metro

World Cup shuttle ridership tops 100,000 rides as fans skip driving to SoFi Stadium

June 12 – June 25, 2026

Metro's dedicated $1.75 shuttle service to SoFi Stadium has logged more than 100,000 rides across the World Cup's first four matches, with ridership climbing every match day. Nearby rail lines are seeing even sharper spikes: the K Line and C Line both posted double-digit ridership jumps on match days, and fare-gate taps at the Expo/USC station rose nearly 600% on the day of the U.S. vs. Paraguay opener.

Key Facts

  • Over 100,000 rides logged to and from SoFi Stadium across the first four World Cup matches
  • Direct shuttle fare: $1.75 per ride, same as a standard bus fare, running from 13 regional hubs every 10 minutes
  • Shuttle ridership grew from 18,551 rides (U.S. vs. Paraguay opener) to over 29,000 rides (Iran vs. Belgium)
  • K Line ridership up 41% and C Line up 23% during the Iran vs. New Zealand match versus a typical Monday
  • Expo/USC station taps up nearly 600% on the U.S. vs. Paraguay match day; D Line ridership up more than 95% during the Mexico vs. South Korea match

Why it matters: The granular, line-by-line numbers show World Cup transit demand isn't just a single SoFi shuttle story — it's lifting ridership across Metro's entire rail network on match days, real-world proof points the agency will lean on heavily while planning for the much larger 2028 Olympics.

MEDIUM SCAG LA Metro

State orders West Hollywood's K Line stations back onto the SB 79 transit-density map

June 22 – June 27, 2026

California's Department of Housing and Community Development directed SCAG on June 22 to restore West Hollywood's three planned K Line Northern Extension stations to the official SB 79 transit-density map, after SCAG had excluded them for lacking completed environmental clearance. HCD ruled that a project only needs an approved "locally preferred alternative" route to qualify — not finished environmental review — forcing SCAG to revise its methodology ahead of a July 2 vote, one day after SB 79 takes effect statewide.

Key Facts

  • HCD issued its directive to SCAG on June 22, 2026, rejecting the environmental-clearance requirement
  • K Line Northern Extension is "Item 25" on SCAG's map corrections, headed for a July 2, 2026 Regional Council vote — one day after SB 79 takes effect
  • Three affected station areas, all on Santa Monica Boulevard: San Vicente, Fairfax, and La Brea
  • SB 79 sets minimum height floors near qualifying stops: 65 feet within a quarter mile, 55 feet within a half mile
  • City staff confirmed over 1,100 rent-stabilized units could be affected without a local Transit-Oriented Development Alternative Plan (TODAP); 38 of 39 speakers at a May 18 council meeting urged adopting one

Why it matters: The last-minute reversal — one day before SB 79 takes statewide effect — shows how contested and procedurally fluid California's new transit-density mandate remains, with thousands of units' worth of zoning hanging on a single mapping methodology dispute.
