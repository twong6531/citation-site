Summary

Cities face a significant challenge regarding climate change, both as major contributors and as highly vulnerable areas. Cities consume 75% of natural resources and generate 60-80% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Furthermore, 70% of urban areas worldwide already experience climate change consequences such as extreme flooding, high temperatures, and droughts. We must consider these critical facts when discussing urban development.

Climate change places considerable stress on urban infrastructure, requiring careful planning and design. For example, cities experience increasing extreme rainfall events, which directly contribute to urban flooding and severely damage transport infrastructure, disrupting daily life. Power grids, another vital urban system, show increased fragility and higher rates of incidents due to more frequent and intense climate-related hazards. Climate change implications extend across all infrastructures and urban systems, demanding robust risk management approaches.

To address these challenges, cities implement various adaptation and mitigation strategies. Green infrastructure, like urban transport projects incorporating green elements, offers a key solution for climate change mitigation and resilience in urban areas. This includes assessing potential ecosystem services provided by green urban infrastructure. Adapting urban drainage infrastructure involves comprehensive planning and design that considers future climate change scenarios. Innovative approaches, such as using reinforcement learning for climate adaptation, help manage flooding and transportation disruptions in cities like Copenhagen.

Integrating climate change considerations into all aspects of urban infrastructure planning, design, and management offers a critical path forward. City planners and engineers can build more resilient, sustainable urban environments by proactively addressing climate risks and leveraging green technologies.
